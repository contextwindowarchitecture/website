The producer contract

Building a tool an assembler can consume.

A producer is anything that emits candidate items into a slot: a retriever, a memory store, an MCP server, a state service, a capability policy, the application's policy loader, its conversation. The route lists each as one of seven kinds: retrieval, memory, mcp, state, capability_policy, policy and interaction. The assembler does not know how a producer works. It knows only what the producer hands over, and that handover is the contract. This page is a guide; the rules it cites are normative in the Spec.

The minimum item

Every item must carry eight fields. Without them the assembler cannot place, rank, expire or trace the item, so it excludes the item at admission (R-2). Emit them on every item, every time, even when the value seems obvious. Two slots need one more, whoever produces the item: every interaction.memory item carries expires (R-9), and every evidence.knowledge item its rerank score in relevance (R-13), read as an IEEE 754 double like every number in a snapshot (R-2). An item without one is excluded with missing_field:expires or missing_field:relevance. scope is optional, and the last row says when it matters.

Policy fields and slot defaults

Six more fields describe how an item may be handled: token_budget, variants, conflict_policy, lineage, eligibility and injection_risk. A producer that knows better than the slot default should set them. When it does not, the assembler fills them from the slot's defaults, or from the route's versioned overrides, and the trace lists every field it filled (R-3). These are the published slot defaults; variants defaults to an empty list.

token_budget caps the tokens of the item's rendered body. null, the default, sets no cap of its own; the payload budget and the slot's max_tokens still bound the item (R-3). An item over its cap is reduced whether or not the payload fits: a compressible item to a variant within the cap, or else it is omitted, and a droppable item is omitted. A protected item over its cap is never cut: the assembly refuses with protected_content_over_budget (R-16). eligibility is a description, never code and never blank: the route's slot rules decide what is eligible, and the trace repeats your text for every included occurrence (R-3, R-22).

The tier column is the highest tier your items can claim. Only the route's versioned policy can raise a slot's tier, and an item that claims more than its slot's tier is excluded with tier_upgrade_not_allowed, so a producer cannot protect its own items. In a slot that is protected by default the tier is also the lowest: an item that sets a lower one there is excluded with protected_tier_changed. Anywhere else you may lower an item's tier, say to droppable so it sheds earlier, even in a slot only the route raised to protected (R-16).

conflict_policy matters only inside a conflict group the application declares, and only between the peers that may instruct: the members at the highest authority present. When exactly one peer governs and the rest defer, the deferring ones are excluded with conflict_deferred; any other pattern escalates, and so does a group that would exclude a protected item (R-11). It is how two governing items — an instruction and a steering example, say — resolve without inventing a tier for each. Facts ignore it; route policy decides them.

Producer kinds

The route policy lists each producer under the identity the application authenticated, with one of seven kinds and the slots it may feed; nothing in an item's own fields proves who sent it (R-15). An item in a slot the route does not list for its producer is excluded with producer_slot_not_allowed. Two kinds have no slot rule of their own: policy and interaction may send whatever slots the route lists for them, except the state slots, which take only kind state (R-8), and governance.capabilities, which takes only the capability policy's grant (R-15). An interaction producer stamps each prior turn's freshness with the instant it was said, since turns render in that order (R-7), and compacts old turns in blocks rather than one per call, so the history's leading part repeats from call to call. On a route whose history slot sets supersede: source or dedupe: exact, turns that share a source, or repeat an earlier reply, are dropped (R-25, R-24). The other five are below, each with the slots its kind allows whatever the route lists, its allowed authority, and the rule most often broken.

the batchwhat every producer hands over
batch Whatever its kind, a producer hands over one batch per call: { items, excluded }, as the producer-batch schema defines it. The application adds producer: { id, kind } when it freezes the snapshot: the identity it authenticated for the batch, never read from item fields (R-15).
items Your candidate items, each admitted or excluded on its own. An entry that is not a JSON object is not an item: the snapshot holding it is rejected (R-2, R-17). Keep ids unique across items and excluded; a candidate sharing one is excluded (R-2).
excluded One row per entry you suppressed: { item_id, reason, stage: "producer" }, with reason one of the exclusion codes below. A duplicate_content row carries duplicate_of and a superseded row superseded_by, no other row carries either, and each names a candidate in the same batch (R-9, R-13).
rejection A snapshot holds at most one batch per producer (R-15). A second batch from the same producer, an excluded row whose duplicate_of or superseded_by names no candidate in its batch, or a reason that is not an exclusion code rejects the whole snapshot before assembly, with no payload and no trace (R-17; conformance README, Snapshot checks).
stage Your rows reach the trace as reported, at stage: producer and with no slot, ahead of the assembler's rows, even from a batch whose producer the route does not admit (R-9). Every other row is the assembler's own, at stage: assembler.
Retrieval → knowledge · tool_results
One scored item per chunk, never a blob.

Emit one evidence item per chunk, each with its rerank score and source version, and set source to the document the chunk comes from, so chunks from one document share it. The retriever owns rewrite, retrieve, rerank and package, and any near-duplicate filtering, reporting each chunk it drops as duplicate_content with duplicate_of naming the chunk it kept, a candidate in the same batch. The assembler then admits each chunk against the route's scope, age and min_relevance, applies exact deduplication and the per-source cap where the route asks for them, and fits what is left to the budget. Every evidence.knowledge item, from any producer, carries relevance, or it is excluded with missing_field:relevance; a score equal to min_relevance passes. An item it sends to any slot but the two evidence slots is excluded with producer_slot_not_allowed, whatever the route lists (R-13). An empty result set is a valid, recorded outcome, so do not pad it. Nor over-fill it: send no more chunks than the route's budget can use, since every chunk the assembler sheds for budget costs a count of the whole payload.

Memory → interaction.memory
The state-versus-memory distinction, expressed as fields.

Every entry carries expires, an absolute deadline, not a duration: an entry whose expires is at or before assembly_time is expired (R-9). Its source identifies the turn it was derived from; a route may set the slot's source_prefix, such as turn:, and an entry whose source lacks it is excluded with source_invalid (R-3). Omit lineage and the slot default, summarised, is filled in. The producer doesn't decide what the user has contradicted; the application revokes the entry, and the store records revoked_by. Expired or revoked entries are never emitted as candidates: report each in the batch's excluded list with reason expired or revoked and stage: producer, and the assembler merges those rows into its trace. An item sent to any slot but interaction.memory is excluded with producer_slot_not_allowed, whatever the route lists (R-14).

Role authority: generated | untrusted · R-14 · R-9
State → state.user · state.task
Owned by the application, and either current or absent.

A state producer reads the database or the workflow engine and emits one item per fact, typed, with freshness set to the read time. It never derives a value from conversation. Claims the user made about themselves go to memory, not state. The route must list it with kind: state: a state item from any other kind of producer is excluded with producer_slot_not_allowed, whatever slots the route lists for it, and an item older than the slot's max_age_seconds is excluded as stale_state. state.task is protected by default: never shed, and an item that sets a lower tier there is excluded with protected_tier_changed, while a state.task item over its own token_budget, or state.task items over the slot's max_tokens, refuse the whole assembly with protected_content_over_budget. state.user is droppable by default: shed first under budget pressure, and an item that claims a higher tier is excluded with tier_upgrade_not_allowed (R-16).

Role authority: state · R-8
MCP → tool_results · knowledge
Results go to Evidence; specifications are proposed, never placed.

Tool results and resources become evidence.tool_results or evidence.knowledge with authority: observation or reference_only, a freshness, and injection_risk: untrusted_content unless the route lists the server with verified: true. That flag is the route's to grant, never the adapter's (R-10), and it does nothing else: it never ranks the server's claims in a fact group (R-11). A resource bound for knowledge needs a relevance score like any retrieved chunk. Where the route sets supersede: source on tool results, only the newest observation per source survives (R-25), so give repeated reads of the same thing the same source. A resource that contains imperatives is still evidence. Tool specifications are proposed to the route's capability policy; the adapter never writes to governance.capabilities itself. An item it emits there is excluded with capability_not_allowed where the route lists that slot for the adapter, and with producer_slot_not_allowed otherwise, as is anything it sends to a slot other than the two evidence slots, whatever the route lists (R-15).

Role authority: observation | reference_only · R-15 · R-10
Capability policy → governance.capabilities
Owned by the application, and the only producer for this slot.

The capability policy holds the versioned allow-list of tools per route and user. It takes proposed specifications from MCP adapters and local tool registries, admits the ones this user may use, and emits one item per tool with authority: governing, trust: verified and injection_risk: none; a governance item that is not verified, or is marked untrusted_content, is excluded with untrusted_in_governance (R-10). The allow-list makes a natural source, and its version with the specification's content hash a natural source_version. The route lists the policy with kind: capability_policy, and the snapshot carries its grant, capabilities: { policy_producer, allow_list_version, allowed_ids }. An item in governance.capabilities is admitted only when its batch comes from the grant's policy_producer, the route lists that producer with kind capability_policy, and allowed_ids names the item's id; any other is excluded with capability_not_allowed (R-15). The slot is protected by default, so an item that sets a lower tier there is excluded with protected_tier_changed (R-16). Permission is decided here, outside the model; the model is told what it may call, and the tool guard still enforces it.

Role authority: governing · R-15 · R-5
Untrusted content
Every item in an evidence slot, in memory, in history and in the query carries injection_risk: untrusted_content, whoever produced it: retrieved chunks, tool output, memory and user turns alike. The slot default fills it when you omit it, and an item that sets none is excluded with untrusted_content_unmarked. The one exception is output from an MCP server the route lists with verified: true, which only the authenticated route policy can grant (R-10, R-15). The marker never changes authority: the query keeps its user authority although it is marked.
Precomputed variants
Compression variants and reranker scores are the producer's job, computed before assembly, and they should be cached by content hash. The assembly step never calls a model (R-18). If your producer can offer a shorter form of an item, emit it as a variant on the item, not as a second item. Each variant has its own id, distinct from its parent's and its siblings' (or the item is excluded with duplicate_variant_id), a body, a method and a lineage. It inherits the parent's provenance, scope and authority, and must not add an instruction or a fact the parent doesn't support. The assembler picks a variant only when its rendered body is shorter than the item's current one. An item over its token_budget takes its longest variant within the cap, and a compressible item with none is omitted (R-16; conformance README, Fitting).

Admission failures

Every exclusion lands in the trace as excluded[] { item_id, reason, stage }, plus duplicate_of or superseded_by when the reason calls for it. The stage says who decided. Producer rows are the ones a batch reported, copied as reported with no slot, listed first and taken from every batch, admitted or not (R-9). Assembler rows are the assembler's own decisions, and carry the item's slot when it names one of the eleven (R-22). When an item fails several checks, the assembler records the earliest code in this list, and among several missing fields the alphabetically first (R-21). These are the reasons a producer will see, and what they mean. The list is generated from contract/reasons.json, which also holds the assembly-level refusal reasons.
