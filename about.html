About

A specification, revised in the open.

Context Window Architecture is a specification for assembling every model call from typed slots. It is not a library, not a framework, and not a product. Anyone may implement it; nobody needs permission.

This page assumes you know:
CWA is
A vocabulary: four planes, eleven slots, one item shape. Twenty-six numbered requirements, and published cases that test what an assembler decides. A contract producers can build to without knowing the assembler. A trace format that makes assembly decisions comparable across implementations.
CWA is not
A prompt template or a recommended order. Placement is a profile you evaluate. A replacement for your agent framework. It fills the blank the framework leaves. A security boundary. Governance items instruct; the application enforces. A claim about how models behave. The Placement page cites what is measured and stops there.
Where it fits

It augments what you already use.

Your existing tools supply the materials, and CWA is the assembly step between them and the model.

Maintainers & revision process

How the spec changes.

The specification is maintained by its authors in the contextwindowarchitecture organisation on GitHub. The specification is written in the contextwindowarchitecture/contextwindowarchitecture repository: SPEC.md, the normative text, with its schemas, contract data, conformance cases and examples, and its issues, pull requests and discussions. This site takes a copy of it at a commit and renders it: the Spec page says what SPEC.md says, and the assembler status matrix comes from its requirements and the implementations' reports. Published schemas define JSON field shapes. Automated checks reject stale generated artifacts.

Requirement numbers are permanent. A withdrawn rule keeps its number and is marked withdrawn. New rules take the next number, whatever section they land in.

Where the draft stands

Try the draft. Four assemblers pass every published case.

Name the slots you already send, give every item an authority and a source, and see how much of your payload had no owner. Draft of 2026-10-07. Four assemblers, in Python, TypeScript, Go and Rust, each pass all 90 published cases, rejections included; none has a versioned release yet, and we want to hear what they must do. That report is what an assembler's conformance claim rests on, while a producer's or an application's rests on its word, and an application must render with a renderer the published cases cover (§1).
